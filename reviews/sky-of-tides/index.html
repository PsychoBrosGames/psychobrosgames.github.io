The thirty-second version

A story-first journey across a literally broken world.

The world broke apart. Great news for anyone who hates normal maps.

Sky of Tides has a world concept strong enough to carry a narrative RPG: oceans in the sky and nine realms hanging over a molten core. Its success depends on choices leaving visible marks rather than only changing the tone of the next conversation.

Okay, what is this thing?

Rin D'Lorah travels through numen9, a shattered world made of nine floating realms above the Inner Sun. The story unfolds across nine fully voiced episodes.

Dialogue, discovery, and risk choices are designed to shape both Rin and the world around her. The game presents itself as an episodic journey where exploration and identity carry more weight than combat spectacle.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • The world consists of nine floating realms above a molten inner sun.
  • The narrative is organized into nine fully voiced episodes.
  • Dialogue, discovery, and risk choices leave marks on the protagonist and world.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

A voiced episode structure is easy to approach.

Clear chapter breaks can make a large fantasy world manageable. The game still needs to explain its terminology without turning every conversation into lore homework.

02

The Descent Guy

Consequences must be more than alternate dialogue.

Risk and discovery choices are interesting when they change later options, relationships, or locations. The system needs enough memory to reward deliberate role-playing.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Nine episodes give the campaign a useful shape.

The format creates natural stopping points for busy players. A strong central performance and visible world change could keep the group talking between chapters.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Narrative RPG players who prioritize world and character
  • Players who prefer episodic stopping points
  • Science-fantasy fans drawn to unusual geography

Dad caveats

  • Lore density in early episodes
  • How materially choices change later content
  • Pacing across nine chapters

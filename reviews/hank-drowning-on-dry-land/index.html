Quick verdict

Thirty minutes of poisoned cartoon noir.

Hank is short enough to commit fully to its premise without turning the subconscious into a collectible map. Its dark Saturday-morning-cartoon style and half-hour target make it a useful reminder that a complete game does not need to fill a calendar.

What it is

Hank is a time-traveling vigilante who has been poisoned and left drowning in spilled beer. The attempt to survive sends him through his own subconscious rather than a conventional crime scene.

The official description targets roughly thirty minutes and frames the presentation like a dark Saturday-morning cartoon. That brevity makes tone, pacing, and a clean ending more important than mechanical expansion.

What stands out

  • The story follows a poisoned vigilante traveling through his subconscious.
  • The visual tone is modeled after dark Saturday-morning cartoons.
  • The official description targets a roughly 30-minute runtime.

The three-player read

One game. Three save files.

01

The Casual

Half an hour is a feature.

A short runtime removes the usual hesitation around starting a narrative game. The experience needs only clear interaction and readable story beats to justify the evening.

02

The Tactician

There is no campaign system to optimize.

The appeal is structure, symbolism, and pacing rather than mastery. Players looking for deep mechanics should treat this as an interactive short, not a compact RPG.

03

The All-Rounder

A complete idea can fit between larger games.

The unusual death and cartoon-noir framing make Hank easy to remember. If the ending lands, the short format is exactly the right size.

Who this game fits

Best for

  • Players who want a complete story in one sitting
  • Fans of surreal noir and dark cartoon aesthetics
  • Anyone looking for a break between longer campaigns

Watch for

  • Limited mechanical depth by design
  • Whether the surreal imagery stays narratively coherent
  • Value expectations around a very short runtime

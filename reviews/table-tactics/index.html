The 30-second dad verdict

Finally, a strategy game where knocking things over counts as planning.

Miniatures strategy where gravity writes the damage numbers.

Table Tactics replaces invisible arithmetic with visible consequences. Weight, shape, position, and momentum decide whether a miniature stays upright, giving every move the clarity of a tabletop stunt and the possibility of an unexpected chain reaction.

Okay, what is this thing?

The battlefield is a tabletop populated by collectible miniature figures. Archers, catapults, mages, traps, marbles, and other tools do not reduce a health meter; they apply force until an opposing figure falls.

Because weight and shape matter, roster building is partly about physical properties. Coins earned through play can be used to buy and trade miniatures, giving the physics puzzle a collection layer.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Victory comes from physically knocking miniatures over instead of depleting health.
  • Unit weight, shape, position, and applied force all affect outcomes.
  • Coins support buying, trading, and building custom miniature rosters.

The dad tribunal

Three dads enter. Agreement remains unlikely.

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Falling over is the clearest damage number in games.

The win condition needs almost no explanation, and a lucky ricochet is entertaining even before the player understands the roster. Precision controls are the accessibility test.

02

The Descent Guy

Physics makes positioning matter twice.

A unit's tactical role and physical body both belong in the plan. The system can reward setup, angle, force, and roster construction without hiding the result behind a calculation.

03

Normal-ish Dad

This has argument-generating potential.

Visible outcomes make the game easy to watch and discuss. If turns stay brisk, Table Tactics could work as both a solo strategy game and a reliable pass-the-controller event.

Who should play and what to watch for

Invite these people

  • Tabletop players who enjoy measuring angles and consequences
  • Strategy fans tired of opaque damage formulas
  • Game-night groups that value readable, surprising outcomes

Dad caveats

  • Control precision when aiming small forces
  • Physics consistency across unit shapes
  • How acquisition and trading affect roster balance

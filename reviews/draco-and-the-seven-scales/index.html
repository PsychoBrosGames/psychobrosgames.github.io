A pirate map that rewards taking the long route.

Draco and the Seven Scales makes a strong case for putting the ship back into pirate adventure. Different sea routes open different dungeons, items, and puzzles, giving travel a strategic purpose beyond connecting islands.

Okay, what is this thing?

Draco is a pirate captain trying to save what matters most to him. The adventure moves between top-down exploration on foot and travel by ship across a large pixel-art world.

Route choice matters because different passages lead to different dungeons, puzzles, and items. Rival captains, armadas, and sea monsters turn those routes into more than scenery.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Exploration alternates between on-foot adventuring and sailing.
  • Different sea routes unlock distinct dungeons, items, and puzzles.
  • Boss threats include rival captains, armadas, and sea monsters.

The dad tribunal

Three dads enter. Agreement remains unlikely.

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The familiar top-down language helps.

Movement, dungeons, and bosses are easy to understand. The ship layer should add possibility without turning basic navigation into a tax.

02

The Descent Guy

Navigation choices need lasting consequences.

Multiple routes are strategically meaningful when they change resources, equipment, and future options. If every path is eventually mandatory, the map becomes a checklist.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A strong genre shape with one useful twist.

Pixel-art adventure is familiar territory, but meaningful sailing can distinguish this from another dungeon run. The balance between land and sea will decide its pace.

Who should play and what to watch for

Invite these people

  • Players who like classic top-down adventure structure
  • Explorers who want route choice to unlock different content
  • Pirate-game fans who expect the ship to matter

Dad caveats

  • Travel time between destinations
  • Whether alternate routes remain genuine choices
  • The balance between exploration, puzzles, and boss combat

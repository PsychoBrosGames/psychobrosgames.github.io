Quick verdict

Retro FPS aggression with a roguelite memory problem.

Moros Protocol combines the direct feedback of a classic shooter with the uncertainty of a roguelite. Crunchy pixel textures and low-poly spaces give The Orpheus a strong look, while augments and found weapons make each return to its shifting corridors a new loadout problem.

What it is

The player wakes without memory aboard the derelict warship The Orpheus. Its corridors shift between runs, and the route out is filled with monsters and demanding bosses.

Combat draws on shooters from the 1990s and early 2000s. Found weapons and augments shape each loadout, adding run variation without replacing the need for movement and aim.

What stands out

  • The Orpheus changes its corridor layout between runs.
  • Low-poly environments are paired with crunchy pixel textures.
  • Weapons and augments shape loadouts across repeated attempts.

The three-player read

One game. Three save files.

01

The Casual

Retro speed needs modern recovery options.

Readable enemies and adjustable difficulty can open the ship to players without classic FPS reflexes. Repeated early rooms should not become a punishment for learning.

02

The Tactician

Augments should change movement and target priority.

The roguelite layer matters when a weapon or augment changes how rooms are approached. Minor damage bumps would not justify the repeated structure.

03

The All-Rounder

A strong run can fit where a campaign mission cannot.

The format is useful for short, intense sessions. Boss checkpoints and progression pacing will determine whether failure encourages another attempt or ends the night.

Who this game fits

Best for

  • Retro FPS players who want procedural replay
  • Roguelite fans who prefer mechanical execution to deck building
  • Science-fiction horror fans drawn to derelict ships

Watch for

  • Repetition in early-run encounters
  • Accessibility for players outside the retro FPS audience
  • How dramatically augments change a run

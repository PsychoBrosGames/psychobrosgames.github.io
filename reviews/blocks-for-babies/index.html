The 30-second dad verdict

Tetris got a shotgun. We have follow-up questions.

Two genres collide hard enough to make the gimmick work.

Blocks for Babies has a pitch that survives the elevator ride: stack the board, jump into it, and shoot the mess you made. The deciding factor will be whether upgrades turn that contrast into strategy instead of asking players to alternate between two disconnected minigames.

Okay, what is this thing?

The run switches between arcade block-stacking and first-person shooting. Clearing lines shapes the 2D board; entering the 3D space lets the player destroy blocks directly with weapons.

Items, weapons, and upgrades are designed to create cross-mode synergies, while bosses test whether the player understands both rule sets. It is a roguelite built around a mechanical punchline with real room to deepen.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Runs alternate between 2D block-stacking and 3D first-person combat.
  • Weapons, items, and upgrades can create synergies across both modes.
  • Boss encounters are designed to test mastery of stacking and shooting together.

The dad tribunal

Three dads enter. Agreement remains unlikely.

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The concept explains itself before the title does.

Most players already understand blocks and shooting. That makes the first minute approachable even if the roguelite upgrade layer becomes more demanding later.

02

The Descent Guy

The best runs should begin on the stacking screen.

The design gets interesting when block placement creates opportunities for the combat phase. If upgrades only raise damage, the two-mode structure loses its strategic reason to exist.

03

Normal-ish Dad

This is built for one-more-run handoffs.

Short, visible mistakes and an unusual premise make it easy to pass around. Long-term appeal depends on bosses and build variety keeping the joke from becoming routine.

Who should play and what to watch for

Invite these people

  • Players who like genre mashups with an immediate arcade loop
  • Roguelite fans who enjoy discovering odd upgrade combinations
  • Game-night spectators who want readable failures and quick resets

Dad caveats

  • Whether the two modes meaningfully affect each other
  • Run variety beyond the first strong gimmick
  • How much downtime sits between stacking and shooting

The thirty-second version

Monster-taming depth in a world already losing the fight.

Catch monsters, fight oblivion, remember which dad named them all.

Voidsayer frames collection as survival rather than sport. Gear, evolution, and a roster of more than one hundred monsters create obvious team-building depth, while procedural events aim to keep the dark world from becoming a fixed route.

Okay, what is this thing?

The Abyss is consuming the world, and the player's answer is a roster of tameable monsters. More than one hundred creatures can be recruited, equipped, and evolved.

The world is interconnected but uses procedural events and challenges to vary the journey. That puts pressure on team flexibility rather than a single solved party.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • The roster contains more than 100 tameable monsters.
  • Monsters can equip gear and evolve.
  • Procedural events and challenges vary the interconnected world.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

A hundred monsters need excellent comparison tools.

Collection is inviting, but gear and evolution can quickly create menu overload. Clear recommendations and readable roles would keep experimentation from feeling like bookkeeping.

02

The Descent Guy

Team construction is the campaign.

Gear, evolution, and procedural challenges give the roster multiple reasons to change. The strongest encounters should punish a universal team without demanding endless grinding.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The dark tone separates it from a crowded field.

Voidsayer is for players ready to invest in a roster. Procedural variation can help the campaign stay fresh if the story still provides memorable places and milestones.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Monster-taming fans who want equipment and evolution depth
  • RPG players comfortable rebuilding teams for new challenges
  • Players drawn to darker worlds and procedural variation

Dad caveats

  • Menu clarity across a large roster
  • Grinding requirements for alternate teams
  • Whether procedural events remain narratively memorable

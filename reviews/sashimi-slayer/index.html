The 30-second dad verdict

We came for the sword controller and stayed because our arms hurt.

The sword controller is the hook; timing is the game.

Sashimi Slayer is exactly the kind of concept that belongs on a show floor: visible, physical, and understandable from across the aisle. The open-source sword is more than a prop if the same parry-and-cut timing remains satisfying on a standard controller.

Okay, what is this thing?

This rhythm-action arcade game turns seafood duels into timed parries and cuts. Hand-drawn art and original music frame three levels built around learning when to defend and when to strike.

Its defining feature is an open-source physical sword controller. That hardware makes the game a performance, while the underlying timing still has to support players using conventional input.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Combat centers on rhythm-based parries and clean cuts.
  • The physical sword controller design is open source.
  • The game includes three levels with hand-drawn art and original music.

The dad tribunal

Three dads enter. Agreement remains unlikely.

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Swinging a sword explains the assignment.

The physical controller turns timing into a visible motion. Standard controls should preserve that clarity for players who meet the game away from an event.

02

The Descent Guy

Precision and pattern depth decide the ceiling.

Three levels can support mastery if enemy sequences demand more than memorization. Clean scoring feedback is essential for a rhythm game with a physical input novelty.

03

Normal-ish Dad

This is a spectator game by design.

Short rounds, physical movement, and readable failure make Sashimi Slayer ideal for handoffs. Its long-term value depends on replay scoring and controller availability.

Who should play and what to watch for

Invite these people

  • Arcade players who enjoy rhythm and score chasing
  • Events or parties with room for the physical controller
  • Makers interested in an open-source game peripheral

Dad caveats

  • Content depth across three levels
  • Parity between sword and standard controls
  • Setup space and hardware availability
